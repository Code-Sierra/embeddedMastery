// 36 — PROFILING LAB

Profile and optimise a 1 kHz firmware frame

Target: STM32F746 (Cortex-M7) at 216 MHz, 7 flash wait states, caches off as after a typical CubeMX project where nobody called SCB_EnableICache(). Every 1 ms the frame runs filter() → crc32() → parse() → display() → log(), then idles; a 10 kHz ADC interrupt pre-empts whatever is running. Budget = 216 000 cycles per frame. Attach instruments, apply optimisations, and compare the Amdahl prediction with what the model actually delivers.

HOW TO USE THIS LAB — GUIDED EXPERIMENTS
  1. Look before you touch. With nothing applied, choose the Sampling profiler instrument. Notice that almost 40 % of samples land in SPI_WaitTXE and UART_WaitTXE — busy-wait loops, not computation. That is your first clue that -O2 will disappoint.
  2. Apply -O2 first. The menu claims "2.5× on all code". Amdahl's prediction uses that claim; the actual result is much smaller because waiting time does not shrink with the compiler. Read the narration.
  3. Apply the CRC look-up table while caches are still off. Notice the actual gain is below the claim: the 1 KB table lives in flash and every table read stalls on 7 wait states. Now enable caches and watch the CRC row shrink again.
  4. Apply DMA for the display, then remove printf and batch the logging. CPU load collapses, and energy per frame falls because the core can sleep (WFI) instead of spinning.
  5. Switch on the RTOS trace instrument and compare its overhead row with the DWT instrument's overhead. Instruments are not free; the lab shows how much each one costs.

// 36 — CURRENT-TRACE ANALYSER

Integrate charge, attribute energy, find power bugs

A synthetic 2-second current trace of a BLE sensor node (nRF52840-class SoC, 3.0 V supply): deep sleep at ~3 µA, an advertising event every 500 ms (CPU wake + three radio bursts on channels 37/38/39) and a sensor read every second. Drag the two cursors (or use the buttons) to integrate charge between them. Change the instrument's sample rate and range to see how a bad measurement setup lies. Inject power bugs, or press Mystery bug and diagnose it from the trace alone.

HOW TO USE THIS ANALYSER — GUIDED EXPERIMENTS
  1. Read the healthy trace. Press Cursors → one adv event. The readout shows the charge of a single advertising event in µC and the energy in µJ. Multiply by 2 events/s and compare with the sleep floor: which dominates the average?
  2. Switch to linear Y axis. The 3 µA sleep floor vanishes into the zero line. That is why current is plotted on a log axis.
  3. Set sample rate to 1 kS/s. The 350 µs radio bursts are now sometimes missed and sometimes over-counted: the measured charge error is shown in red. Set the range to "fixed 0–100 mA, 12-bit": the sleep current is quantised to 0 or 24 µA.
  4. Inject "debugger attached". The floor jumps to ~1 mA and battery life collapses from years to days. Remove it and inject "SysTick not suppressed" — look for a picket fence of 1 ms spikes.
  5. Press Mystery bug. A hidden bug is injected. Use cursors, average current and the event table to decide which bug it is, then choose your diagnosis.
